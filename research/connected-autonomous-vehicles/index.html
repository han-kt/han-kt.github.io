Research / Research area

Cooperative Driving Automation & V2X

Connected vehicles can share their state and intent over vehicle-to-vehicle and vehicle-to-cloud links. This work asks how that shared information can support cooperative maneuvers such as merging, intersection crossing, and platooning; how cooperation holds up when messages are delayed or lost; and how such systems can be evaluated beyond simulation. It develops cooperative maneuver strategies, communication-aware methods, and the co-simulation platforms and vehicle tests used to evaluate them.

Main themes

  1. Cooperative ramp merging

    Merging based on digital twins of vehicles and drivers kept on a cloud server over 4G/LTE, with advisories returned to drivers and a field implementation on three passenger vehicles; and game-theoretic merging for mixed traffic of automated and human-driven vehicles.

    Liao et al., IEEE T-ITS 2022 · Liao et al., IEEE T-SMC 2022

  2. Intersections and roundabouts

    Digital-twin-assisted cooperative driving at non-signalized intersections and at roundabouts.

    Wang et al., IEEE T-IV 2022 · Li et al., IEEE FISTS 2024

  3. Cooperation over imperfect communication

    Intent communication between vehicles to improve platooning, validated with prototype vehicles; motion estimation under V2X communication delay and packet loss; and identifying the sender of vehicle-to-vehicle messages.

    Sharma et al., IEEE INFOCOM 2026 (poster) · Wang et al., SAE WCX 2021 · Kobayashi et al., IEEE VTC 2019

  4. Co-simulation platforms

    Platforms that couple the Unity game engine with SUMO traffic simulation to model and evaluate connected and automated vehicles in mixed traffic; the 2019 ramp-merging simulation article received the SAE JCAV Best Paper Award.

    Wang et al., SAE JCAV 2019 · Zhao et al., SAE JCAV 2022

Systems and validation

  • Field implementation of cooperative ramp merging with three passenger vehicles in Riverside, California. (Liao et al., IEEE T-ITS 2022)
  • Platooning with intent communication validated on prototype vehicles. (Sharma et al., IEEE INFOCOM 2026 (poster))
