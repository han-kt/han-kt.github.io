Research / Research area

Digital Twins and Cooperative Mobility

Research question

How can shared digital representations help vehicles coordinate? Collaborative research connects vehicle, driver, and traffic models with vehicle-to-cloud communication, develops cooperative maneuver methods, and builds platforms for studying mixed traffic. Evaluations range from Unity–SUMO co-simulation to controlled passenger-vehicle field studies of ramp-merging advisories.

Contributions Visual overviewEvidence Publications

Methods & contributions

  1. Cooperative ramp merging with connected digital twins

    Vehicle and driver digital twins support ramp-merging advisories over cellular vehicle-to-cloud links. A related game-theoretic method studies mixed automated and human-driven traffic with Unity–SUMO co-simulation.

    Liao et al., IEEE T-ITS 2022 · Liao et al., IEEE T-SMC 2022 · Wang et al., SAE JCAV 2019

  2. Cooperative intersections and roundabouts

    Digital-twin-assisted methods extend cooperative-driving studies to non-signalized intersections and roundabouts. These investigate different maneuver geometries through simulation and co-simulation.

    Wang et al., IEEE T-IV 2022 · Li et al., IEEE FISTS 2024

  3. Architecture and experimental platforms

    Mobility Digital Twin organizes human, vehicle, and traffic twins on a cloud–edge–device architecture. Unity and Unity–SUMO platforms support simulation and co-simulation of connected vehicles and mixed traffic.

    Wang et al., IEEE IoT-J 2022 · Wang et al., IEEE DTPI 2021 · Zhao et al., SAE JCAV 2022

  4. Identifying senders and estimating motion over imperfect links

    V2V sender identification associates messages with surrounding vehicles, while motion estimation accounts for communication delay and packet loss.

    Kobayashi et al., IEEE VTC 2019 · Wang et al., SAE WCX 2021

  5. Cooperative control and exchanged intent

    Lookup-table consensus studies longitudinal coordination, while a separate prototype-vehicle study examines platooning with communicated intent. Edge-assisted misbehavior detection investigates another vulnerability of platoon coordination.

    Wang et al., ACC 2019 · Sharma et al., IEEE INFOCOM 2026 (poster) · Kan et al., IEEE VNC 2019

  6. Making connected information available to drivers

    Augmented-reality assistance explores how connected-vehicle information can be presented to a driver.

    Wang et al., IEEE SMC 2020

Visual overview

Systems & evidence

  • Cooperative ramp merging was implemented with passenger vehicles over a cellular vehicle-to-cloud link in a controlled field study. (Liao et al., IEEE T-ITS 2022)
  • Intent-enabled platooning was evaluated with prototype vehicles; digital-twin-assisted intersection work reports a separate simulation study. (Sharma et al., IEEE INFOCOM 2026 (poster); Wang et al., IEEE T-IV 2022)
  • The Mobility Digital Twin architecture uses personalized ACC as a case study connecting human, vehicle, and traffic representations with assistance. (Wang et al., IEEE IoT-J 2022)
