Research / Research area

Mobility & Driver Digital Twins

Driver-assistance systems are usually tuned for an average driver, while individual drivers differ in car following, lane changes, and stop-or-go decisions. This work asks how to keep synchronized models of drivers, vehicles, and traffic, and how to use them to predict and accommodate individual behavior. It develops digital-twin frameworks on a cloud–edge–device architecture and learns personalized driver models within them.

Main themes

  1. Mobility Digital Twin architecture

    A data-driven cloud–edge–device architecture with human, vehicle, and traffic digital twins, demonstrated through a personalized adaptive cruise control case study.

    Wang et al., IEEE IoT-J 2022

  2. Driver digital twins

    Online prediction of an individual driver's lane-change behavior and online recognition of distracted driving.

    Liao et al., IEEE IoT-J 2023 · Ma et al., IEEE T-IV 2024

  3. Personalized car following

    Gaussian-process models of individual car-following behavior learned from naturalistic driving data and combined with a predictive safety filter for personalized adaptive cruise control.

    Wang et al., IEEE T-ITS 2022 · Wang et al., IEEE ITSC 2021

  4. Personalization and trust

    A review of personalization in driving behavior covering datasets, modeling, and validation; prediction of personalized stop-or-go decisions in dilemma zones; and planning for automated vehicles that accounts for human trust.

    Liao et al., IEEE T-IV 2025 · Qin et al., IEEE RA-L 2025 · Sheng et al., ACM TCPS 2022

Systems and validation

  • Learning-based models of driver speed-tracking error for advisory speed assistance, tested with 17 drivers on a Unity game-engine driving simulator and further validated in a field implementation. (Wang et al., IEEE T-IV 2020)
  • Digital-twin simulation of connected and automated vehicles with the Unity game engine (Best Application Award). (Wang et al., IEEE DTPI 2021)
